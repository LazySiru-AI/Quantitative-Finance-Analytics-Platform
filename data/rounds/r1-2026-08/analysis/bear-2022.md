# Window analysis — `bear-2022` (2022-01-03 → 2022-10-12, 196 sessions)

Benchmark (SPY, price-only, first open to last close): **−25.1396%** (given figure −25.14%; every
runner's own `benchmark_return` reads −0.2513961788788578… — same figure to the digit shown, no
discrepancy). 19 cards ran in this window: 13 incumbents, 5 challengers, and `c06-13f-momentum`,
unranked by construction and reported separately below.

This is a grinding, rate-driven decline with no single shock day — the regime most trend-following
rules claim to be built for, and the regime that most exposes a rule that only knows how to buy
already-confirmed uptrends. There is no snapback inside this window to reward "the entry filter
happened to not fire" the way `covid` does; a card that mostly sat in cash here sat in cash for real,
and the question of whether that reflects genuine risk management or a rule that simply never
triggered is the central one this window is built to answer.

## A note on `exposure`, corrected mid-task

The `exposure` field in the run JSON (`runner.py:517` / `metrics.py:90`) is **the fraction of
sessions on which the card held at least one open lot, not the fraction of capital deployed.** A
4-slot card holding one small slot every session reports `exposure = 1.0` while only a quarter of
its capital was ever at risk. `adjusted_excess = total_return − exposure × benchmark_return` therefore
**over-credits multi-slot, low-fill cards**, and it does so **worst in exactly this kind of
falling-benchmark window**: the higher the reported exposure, the more of the benchmark's −25.14% gets
subtracted back out of the card's own loss, regardless of how much money was actually at risk.

To answer "how much was at risk," this analysis reconstructs a **capital-weighted exposure** directly
from the trade logs: for each session in the window, sum `quantity × entry_price` over every position
open that session (`entry_date` through `exit_date` inclusive, or through window-end for
`open_at_end`), divide by `starting_equity` (10000.00 for every card), and average across all 196
sessions (business days minus the seven 2022 NYSE holidays that fall inside the window, cross-checked
against the reported session count). Both figures are reported below — `session_exp` (the raw JSON
field, treated as an **upper bound**) and `capital_exp` (the reconstructed figure, used for the
defended-vs-abstained verdict). Where the two diverge by more than 20 points, that is flagged
explicitly. In this window the divergence is larger and more consequential than in `covid`: several
cards' entire positive-`adjusted_excess` story evaporates or reverses once capital exposure replaces
session exposure — see §2 and §6.

## 2. Incumbents vs. challengers

On the raw numbers this reads as a clean challenger win. It is not that clean, for two separate
reasons. First, the design-fairness caveat: the incumbents were built and tuned against a
semiconductor/AI bull market and are being run, unmodified, against a rate-driven grind they were
never designed to survive; the challengers were designed with a crisis window explicitly in the test
matrix. Crediting the challengers with "handling the bear market better" partly just restates that
they knew this window existed and the incumbents did not. Second, and more directly: **once exposure
is capital-weighted, the challenger group's apparent edge nearly disappears.** Mean `adj(cap)` for
challengers is +0.16% — indistinguishable from zero, and built from a mix of small positives and small
negatives (c01 +1.10%, c05-trend +1.37%, c03-breakout +0.30%, c02-volatility −0.45%,
c04-riskmanaged −1.54%). The incumbents remain clearly negative (mean −14.19%), driven almost entirely
by the A-family's repeated whipsaw losses (see §5's discussion of the A-family below and §3). The
honest summary is: the incumbents actively lost money trying to trade a decline their rules were not
built for, while the challengers, once you strip out the mechanical exposure credit, mostly avoided
losing money rather than demonstrating a working edge in it. Neither group should be declared the
"winner" of this window.

## 3. Do the A/B variants separate from their bases?

Each variant isolates one change. Per the brief, treat any gap under **~20 percentage points of
return** as noise (a prior universe-ordering study moved one identical card's return by 53.9 points,
σ = 21.2, from reordering alone — nothing else). This section is unaffected by the exposure
correction, since it compares `total_return` directly.

| base → variant | change isolated | base return | variant return | Δ (pp) | clears 20pp? |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| A-rotation → A1-alpha-order | universe reorder only (control) | −33.80% | −36.23% | −2.43 | no |
| A-rotation → A2-cash-headroom | 4 slots @ 22% vs 3 @ 30% | −33.80% | −29.26% | +4.54 | no |
| A-rotation → A3-no-target | remove 20% take-profit | −33.80% | −33.80% | 0.00 | no |
| A-rotation → A4-tight-stop | stop 12% → 8% | −33.80% | −34.13% | −0.33 | no |
| B-momentum → B1-with-target | add 25% take-profit | −6.77% | −6.77% | 0.00 | no |
| B-momentum → B2-hold-90 | max hold 180d → 90d | −6.77% | −6.77% | 0.00 | no |
| B-momentum → B3-concentrated | 4 slots @ 22% vs 6 @ 15% | −6.77% | −10.17% | −3.40 | no |
| B-momentum → B4-tight-stop | stop 12% → 8% | −6.77% | −5.63% | +1.14 | no |

**None of the eight variant differences clear the 20pp bar in this window either.** A3-no-target is
*bit-for-bit identical* to A-rotation (both −33.80%, 22 trades, all exits `rule`, no stops) — the
20% take-profit this variant removes simply never got a chance to bind in a market that never rallied
that far. B1 and B2 are also identical to B-momentum, for the same reason as in `covid`: neither the
25% target nor the 90-day hold cap ever bound on these five trades. The one variant that did visibly
change *mechanism* without changing *outcome* by a meaningful margin is A4-tight-stop: tightening the
stop from 12% to 8% is the only change in the A-family that actually altered which exit reason fired
(3 genuine stops appear in A4's log; A, A1, A2 and A3 all closed every position via `rule`, never a
stop, in this window) — yet the return effect is −0.33pp, deep inside the noise band. Across both
windows now examined, **zero of sixteen variant-vs-base comparisons clear the 20-point noise floor.**
That consistency across two very different crisis regimes is itself the finding: these parameter
changes do not separate from their bases at the resolution this program can measure.

## 5. Did the trend cards handle the grinding decline?

Discipline `trend` covers three cards here: `C-trend` and `H-trend-diversified` (incumbents) and
`c05-trend` (challenger). `G-volatility` is a `volatility`-discipline card, not trend, and is excluded
from this section despite the name similarity.

**No — not through classic trend-following, and the capital-weighted exposure figures make this
clearer than the raw ones do.**

- **C-trend** traded five times, all closing via `rule`, and stopped entirely after its last exit on
  2022-04-26 — **zero trades for the remaining roughly five and a half months of the window**
  (2022-04-27 through 2022-10-12). Capital exposure across the full 196 sessions is **5.02%**, the
  lowest of any card in the table. Its `adj(cap)` is −5.70%, negative — it did not even beat the
  exposure-scaled benchmark. This is a long-only trend filter that requires a confirmed uptrend to
  enter; in a market with no sustained uptrend to confirm, it produced almost no signal at all, and
  what little it did produce lost money (MU stopped −16.56%, NVDA stopped −14.91%). That is not
  trend-following handling a decline — it is a trend-following rule going quiet because there was
  nothing for it to follow.
- **c05-trend** traded 13 times — more active than C-trend — but its book was dominated by GLD
  (gold), held in several stretches including one 65-session run (2022-02-07 to 2022-05-11, +1.70%),
  plus short-duration SPY round-trips mostly for small losses (−0.32% to −2.49%). Capital exposure is
  21.15%, higher than C-trend but still modest, and its last trade closes 2022-06-21 — **no trades for
  the final three and a half months of the window**. `adj(cap)` is +1.37%, positive but small — close
  to the noise floor and largely attributable to gold's mildly positive year rather than to equity
  trend-following surviving an equity decline.
- **H-trend-diversified** is the partial exception, and the correction changes its story
  substantially. Its headline number benefits from a genuine winning trade: **DBC (commodities),
  entered 2022-01-03, held the full 120-day cap, exited 2022-06-27 via `time`, for +33.14%** — a real
  capture of the 2022 commodity/energy spike that offset stopped-out equity trend positions (MU
  −16.56%, NVDA −14.91%, both `stop`). Read on session exposure alone (81.63%) this looks like a
  strongly engaged, successful trend book; read on capital exposure (**15.59%**), it is clear this was
  one modestly-sized commodity position doing most of the work while the rest of the book was mostly
  empty. `adj(cap)` is **+0.71%** — essentially flat, not the +17.32% the raw exposure field implies.
  The diversification thesis (hold assets that do not trend together) worked exactly as designed — the
  win came from the non-equity leg — but it was a small, single-position win, not evidence of a
  well-capitalized trend sleeve outperforming through the decline.

**Answer:** none of the three trend-discipline cards demonstrate trend-following that "handles" a
grinding decline in the way the design brief for this discipline claims. Two of the three (C-trend,
c05-trend) went quiet for the back half of the window — best read as the entry filter finding nothing
to confirm, not as a risk decision — and the one that finished closest to flat (H-trend-diversified)
did so on the strength of one small commodity position, not on equity trend logic surviving the
equity decline. All three are long-only and cannot express a short view, so "handling" a down-only
regime was structurally out of reach for this discipline as implemented here regardless of the
specific card.

## 6. Defended vs. abstained, per card

This window's benchmark is deeply negative (−25.14%), which makes the mechanical effect described
above strong: almost every card in the table shows large positive raw `excess` simply from not being
fully invested in a falling market. The verdict below rests on **capital-weighted exposure**, per the
correction in §0, not the raw session field.

- **Genuinely defended (meaningful capital at risk, active trading, real losses cut smaller than the
  market's):** c04-riskmanaged (18.66% capital exposure, 18 trades — the most active challenger, a
  diversified TLT/EFA/GLD/SPY sleeve with real gains on GLD offsetting real SPY losses); c03-breakout
  (34.39% capital exposure, 9 trades, including a genuine winner — XLE held 2022-01-07 to 2022-06-24
  for +18.19% — offsetting several stopped-out ETF rotations); c05-trend (21.15% capital exposure, 13
  trades, as discussed in §5). All three land close to flat on `adj(cap)` (+0.30% to +1.37%) —
  real engagement, but no demonstrated edge once exposure is honestly measured.
- **Engaged and actively losing (real capital at risk, real trading, genuine negative skill relative
  to the exposure-scaled benchmark):** the entire A-family — A-rotation, A1-alpha-order,
  A2-cash-headroom, A3-no-target, A4-tight-stop (26.3–26.7% capital exposure, 22–33 trades each, the
  most trades of any cards in the window) — and G-volatility (7.28% capital exposure but 15 trades and
  the worst `adj(cap)` of any ranked card, −18.89%, from stop-outs on COIN and PLTR). These cards were
  not sitting in cash; they were repeatedly re-entering a "confirmed uptrend" that kept failing,
  taking a new small loss (−4% to −11%, typically closed by `rule` before a stop could bind) on nearly
  every attempt. This is the clearest evidence in either window of an entry rule mechanically
  unsuited to its regime, actively trading its way to a worse outcome than simply holding cash.
- **Effectively abstained after early losses (low capital exposure, engagement front-loaded into the
  first quarter, then quiet):** B-momentum, B1-with-target, B2-hold-90, B3-concentrated,
  B4-tight-stop (6.65–10.15% capital exposure, only 5 trades each, all closed by 2022-05-02 — **no
  trades for the remaining five and a half months**); C-trend (5.02% capital exposure, as discussed in
  §5). These cards took two or three real losing trades early (MU and NVDA stop-outs feature in nearly
  all of them) and then generated no further signal for the rest of the grinding decline — `adj(cap)`
  for this group ranges from −3.96% to −7.61%, negative despite very low exposure, meaning even the
  small amount of capital they did commit lost relative to the exposure-scaled benchmark.
- **Close to pure abstention, with a small positive residual:** c01-meanrev (6.76% capital exposure —
  the lowest capital commitment of any card in the window — 8 trades clustered in two short bursts,
  `adj(cap)` +1.10%, the least-negative of anyone but built almost entirely from simply not being in
  the market); c02-volatility (24.93% capital exposure, 4 trades, `adj(cap)` −0.45%, essentially flat).
  c06-13f-momentum (unranked; 11.43% capital exposure, 25 trades — the most trades of any card in the
  window despite modest capital committed, `adj(cap)` −12.74%, actively engaged and losing).

## 7. Limitations

This is one draw from one historical grinding decline. 2022 was specifically a rate-driven,
commodity-inflationary bear market; a different grinding decline (e.g., one without a commodity
tailwind) would likely have removed H-trend-diversified's one genuine winning position and left all
three trend cards essentially flat-to-negative with no exception. `sharpe`, `sortino`, `volatility`
and `profit_factor` are null for every card in this run and are not reported. The A-family's
performance here is a single realization of "buy the confirmed uptrend" meeting a market that kept
producing false starts; a bear market with fewer countertrend rallies (or one with more) would move
this group's numbers substantially, and nothing in this window distinguishes "this rule is badly
suited to bear markets in general" from "this particular bear market produced an unusually high rate
of whipsaws." Finally, the capital-weighted exposure figures in this file are a reconstruction from
trade logs against an inferred NYSE session calendar (business days minus the seven 2022 holidays
falling in-window), cross-checked to reproduce the reported 196-session count exactly — not a value
read from the harness itself.
